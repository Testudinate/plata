Part 1 · Enforcement and ownership

A rule that cannot be skipped, instead of a rule that must be remembered

How the drift is stopped

MechanismWhat it is
M1 Do not rename — translateA mass rename across 20+ schemas breaks Tableau and never starts. Instead a name-resolution layer: old names survive but stop being a fork in the road. Zero migrations
M2 Standard applies at the entry pointMandatory for new objects; existing ones are registered as they are, with an alias. A standard that demands rewriting the past dies in month one
M3 A convention becomes a predicateEach rule is stored as a row with a ready SQL condition: 12 sign rules of the form COR_PRNP <= 0, each citing its source appendix
M4 A gate at the entrance to the layerNo object without a card (grain, filter column, sign), a named owner and steward, an SLA with an action, and a passing check run
M5 Drift is measured, not declaredA DDL snapshot is diffed against the change log. The metric is not "we introduced a standard" but "how many changes shipped unannounced"

Ownership = three separable duties

RoleAccountable for
Data ownerWhat the number means; signs off a change to the definition
Data stewardOperational quality; receives the alert when a check fails
Technical ownerThe pipeline and the load SLA

The owner is whoever bears the consequences of a wrong number, not whoever wrote the pipeline. Operationally that is four obligations, each answerable by query: a signature under the definition · a named duty with a named action · a threshold in the right calendar · an obligation to give notice.

Reality test: who signed the definition, who gets woken and what they do, who had to be told in advance and how many days ahead. While any of the three is answered by a phone call, ownership exists on paper only.

Why a predicate and not a policy document — I tripped over this myself. The first semantic model for Cortex described the mandatory filter statement_num >= 2 in a comment on the metric. A natural-language question returned 14.42% instead of the canonical 14.62%. Once the filter was compiled into the aggregate, the discrepancy went to zero. A comment enforces nothing — and a review policy without an automatic check is the same comment.
Evidence: 26 checks in 15 categories, none failing · ownership 25 of 25 on the risk layer3 / 9

Two findings that reframe the task. The spread has no constant sign: in April the canon is the highest of the three, in March the lowest (Feb 0.74 pp · Mar 2.30 · Apr 3.24 · Jun 4.37). Part 2 · Drift detection against the DQM ladder

Three teams will drift again — the question is who notices first

The detector does not check whether a number is "right": all three are right inside their own methodology. It catches three events.

#SignalWhy it is built this way
D1The bridge residual stopped being zeroThe bridge converges by construction, so a non-zero residual is the only signal that catches an unknown unknown: a calculation changed quietly, a new branch appeared, the data moved
D2A step's contribution left its historical corridorThe grain step ranges from −7.1 to +8.1 pp across 24 months — that is normal, not broken. The threshold is built from each step's own history, or the alert fires on healthy seasonality
D3A team's published number diverged from its registered formulaRecomputed from METRIC_DEFINITIONS and compared with what the team publishes. The only check aimed at people rather than data
Why the sign check is an aggregate, not a row check. Individual positive COR values are legitimate — that is a provision release when an account cures. A row-level rule would fire on every healthy portfolio and be switched off in week one. An alert that gets ignored is worse than no alert, and the same principle sets the D2 thresholds.

The single yellow check is a real incident — stm_cc is 9 business days behind against an SLA of 5. Tellingly, I first put that delay only into the ETL log while the data itself was current, and the traffic light showed all green. The monitor was measuring something other than what it claimed. That is exactly the class of error that makes a DQ dashboard decorative.

Part 3 · Change management

Gen5v1 re-estimated: a data change with no schema change

Nothing fails, no query errors — every dashboard quietly starts showing different numbers, which makes it more dangerous than dropping a table. A drop is noticed in a minute; a 35% shift in EL LT for 2025 H1 vintages is noticed a quarter later, in a variance review.

Three decisions to close before doing anything

DecisionWho decides, and why them
Retrospective
closed periods or forward only		Head of IRM with Finance — monthly reporting breaks on exactly this. The consequence lands on reporting, not on modelling
Form of publication
new snapshot or overwrite		The mart owner. Here: a new 2026-06 snapshot, 2026-03 left alive. That is versioning, not "a backup just in case"
Life of the old base
until when may a consumer stay		The mart owner with the consumer. Without a date the pin is permanent and the organisation keeps two truths forever

Then, in order

  • Measure the shift before announcing it — ×1.35 on EL LT for 2025-01…2025-06 vintages. "The model was re-estimated" without a number is useless.
  • Notify from the registry, version the mart, log the change with a breaking flag and the fact of notice.
  • Keep a rollback path: switching a view, not reloading data — both snapshots stay live, so rollback costs seconds. A plan that needs a restore from backup is never used.
  • Close on evidence, not on the date: retire the old view once access history shows nobody is left on it. While someone still reads the previous version, truth has multiplied rather than migrated.

Impact assessment: a query, not a phone round

ConsumerNoticeWhat actually breaks it
CLIP decision model30 daysThe EL LT shift — the model is calibrated on it
Monthly reporting (CUBE)20 daysValue changes on closed periods; a new snapshot is fine

The agent row matters: a human needs warning in order to rebuild a report; an agent gains nothing from warning — it needs the context layer to update at the same moment as the data.

Part 4 · AI readiness

The appendices are good documentation for a human

An agent lacks three things: structure instead of prose (a row it can find by object name, not a sentence) · term resolution (the glossary says TDC is a credit card, not that in a query it is product_risks = 'CC') · rules inside the data, not beside it (a mandatory filter in a document can be ignored; compiled into the metric it cannot).

Trust is a product, not a sum

privileges × measured correctness at a threshold × answer provenance × the ability to review what it did. Zero any factor and the result is zero, not "partially trusted".

Working definition used throughout: Basic — the practice lives in people's heads · Defined — the rule is written and has an owner · Controlled — compliance is checked automatically and breaches escalate to a name · Continuing — the control system itself is measured.

Not a 90-day priority, deliberately. Reference Data Management (an alias reference closes 90% of the pain in a day) · Master Data Management for customers · replatforming or buying a catalog (a tool bought before the process works preserves the absence of process) · ownership coverage of tier3 (tagging forty technical tables creates the appearance of governance).

DQM and Change Management, Basic → Defined

Not only because the assignment names them the sharpest pain, but for the dependency order: without DQ you cannot prove a change broke something, and without change management any quality gain is rolled back by the next release. In parallel — the canonical metric layer: you cannot monitor the quality of a metric whose definition is not fixed.

GateWhat is doneHow it is evidenced
Day 30Five measurements taken; the metric canon fixed and signed; the bridge between the three calculations computedThe decomposition reproduces from data with a zero residual. Priorities explicitly confirmed or revised
Day 60Checks on every tier1 table with threshold, steward and prescribed action; statement freshness in business days; change log establishedNo Monday false alarms; every firing signal has an addressee
Day 90Consumer registry with notice periods; schema snapshot reconciliation running automaticallyNo breaking change without a record and a notice; reconciliation finds nothing unlogged

What I measure in the first 30 days — two of the five can overturn this plan, and that is the point: how many versions of one metric are actually in circulation · share of incidents found by monitoring before the user (the only one that cannot be measured retroactively, so the incident log with a mandatory "who found it" field is set up in week one) · share of schema changes that were registered · time to answer a typical analyst question · agent pass rate on the golden set.

The main risk of the plan is not technical. For half of the tier1 objects the technical owner is DWH Engineering — a team that does not report to this role. The workaround is built into the design: every check is constructed on the risk side and requires no change to upstream pipelines. Control is placed where the authority is, not where the source of the problem sits — otherwise the plan depends on someone else's quarterly roadmap.
An acceptance criterion without an acceptor is a self-assessment: the Head of IRM accepts against the same three gates, recorded in the registry8 / 9
Part 5 · The team

Two Data Stewards: remove work first, add work second

The two analytics engineers are not idle capacity — a plan that adds work to a saturated queue is not a plan. Managing two people is a capacity decision before it is a people decision.

First two weeks: measure their time, not the data

What I measureWhat decision it changes
Structure of incoming work — ad-hoc / extract / incident / developmentIf more than half is ad-hoc, the context layer moves ahead of DQ checks: it removes work rather than adding it
Repeat rate of questionsA repeated question is a documentation defect, not an analyst request. Fixed once, in the glossary or a card
Interrupt loadTwo people cannot absorb both an ad-hoc queue and a delivery plan
Single points of knowledgeBus factor is a governance risk, not an HR one. It sets the rotation
Where the queue actually stallsSeparates "we are slow" from "we are blocked by DWH" — only one of them is mine to fix
The appendices predict most of the toil: three COR versions mean somebody reconciles by hand before every management meeting; "silently produces incorrect results" means every query is re-verified by attention rather than by a check; broken cross-references mean the same questions answered in chat again and again; weekend-stale means a false alarm triaged every Monday. Every one is toil manufactured by the absence of governance — hence the sequencing rule: the first delivery must give them hours back, or the second never gets built.

Both are named in META.OWNERSHIP as stewards of their own objects, with the action they take when a check fails. The ownership model this response proposes for data is the model I run the team on. Duty rotates weekly; both present at a gate at least once.

How the experience transfers

  • Pair on the first, solo on the rest — the first sign predicate together, the other eleven theirs. The teaching cost is paid once per class of work, not per task.
  • They author the standard, I review it — one written by the lead gets complied with; one written by the steward gets defended. The naming check missing from the 26 is the first thing I hand over rather than close myself.
  • "Explain the number" duty — whoever owns a definition answers the Head of Risk directly, with me in the room and not in front.
  • Review with the reason attached — every rejection names the check that would have caught it. Review that only says no teaches obedience.
How I know the delegation is real: share of checks authored by the stewards rather than by me — near zero at day 30, above two thirds at day 90. Not delegated: the signature under the canon, the right to refuse at the gate, the agent's trust threshold. Never kept: writing all the checks myself — a lead who builds everything personally ends up with two executors and a bus factor of one.
